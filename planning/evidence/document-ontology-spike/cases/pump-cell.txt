# Pump Assembly Cell: Inspection and Release Specification

Revision C, synthetic document written for the Autology document-intelligence spike. It describes no real plant.

## 1 Scope

This specification covers the assembly, inspection and release of centrifugal pump units built in the assembly cell. It applies to every Work Order released to the cell.

## 2 Terms

A Pump Unit is an assembled pump ready for testing.
A Work Order is the instruction to build a given quantity of pump units.
A Non-Conformance Report (NCR) is the record of a defect found during inspection.
A Test Bench is a station where pump units are tested under pressure.

## 3 Structure

A pump unit consists of a casing, an impeller, a shaft seal and a motor.
Each work order has one or more pump units.
Each pump unit belongs to one work order.
Each NCR concerns exactly one pump unit.
A pump unit has several test results.
An NCR is a kind of quality record.

## 4 Life cycle

The status of a pump unit is one of: assembled, tested, released or scrapped.
A work order can be open, in progress, completed or cancelled.
A pump unit moves from tested to released when all test results are within tolerance.
The NCR passes through draft, under review and closed.

## 5 Inspection

The inspector records the test results for each pump unit on the test bench. The hydrostatic test runs at 16 bar for 10 min; leakage above 0.5 ml is a failure. The motor current must stay below 12 A at nominal speed.

The inspector raises a NCR when a test result is out of tolerance.

## 6 Release

A pump unit must be approved by the quality engineer when an NCR is open on it.
Only the quality engineer may close an NCR.
A scrapped pump unit must not be released.
The shift supervisor releases the pump units at the end of each shift.

The inspector records the test results for each pump unit on the test bench. The hydrostatic test runs at 16 bar for 10 min; leakage above 0.5 ml is a failure. The motor current must stay below 12 A at nominal speed.

## 7 References

Testing follows ISO 9906 and the materials certificates follow EN 10204. Calibration records are kept for 5 years.

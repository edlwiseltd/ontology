# Riverside Community Garden: Plot Allocation Rules

Adopted at the members' meeting. A synthetic document for the Autology spike; the garden does not exist.

## Purpose

The committee allocates garden plots fairly and keeps the waiting list honest. These rules explain how a member applies for a plot, how plots are assigned and when a plot is taken back.

## Who is who

Members are people who have paid the annual fee. A plot is a numbered piece of ground of about 25 m², and a half-plot is a kind of plot. The waiting list is the ordered list of applications that could not be satisfied yet.

## How things fit together

The garden is divided into twelve beds, and each bed contains several plots. Each plot is assigned to at most one member at a time. A member has one or more applications over the years. Every application concerns one plot size.

Applications can be received, waiting, offered, accepted or declined.
The status of a plot is one of: free, offered, taken or resting.

## Decisions

The secretary records each application with its date. The committee offers a free plot to the first application on the waiting list. An exchange of plots between two members must be approved by the committee.
Only the treasurer may waive the annual fee.
A plot must never be offered to a member who owes fees.
A plot that stays uncultivated for 3 months is taken back after a written warning.

Plots, plot holders and the plot list are reviewed every January. A plot holder is a member who holds a plot.
